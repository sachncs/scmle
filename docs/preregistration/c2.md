# C2 Leakage caught, clean data not blocked: pre-registration

Registered 2026-11-17. Shared rules: [README.md](README.md). Acceptance
row.

## Criterion

Recall lower bound at least `leak_recall_min` on real leaks (about 60
documented real leaks plus leaks planted in about 100 clean real
datasets), entity-level leaks included; false-block upper bound at most
`false_block_max` on about 150 clean real datasets with strong legitimate
predictors.

## Population and data

- Leak corpus: real public tables whose leak is documented by the source
  (recorded after the outcome, includes the target, and similar), and real
  clean tables with one realistic leak planted: target encoding computed on
  all rows, a post-outcome field, duplicated rows straddling the split, a
  future timestamp, and an entity-level leak (the same entity on both
  sides with an entity-constant feature that encodes the label). One case
  is one (table, leak); each table contributes at most one planted case
  per leak kind.
- Clean corpus: real public tables as published, with the task a
  practitioner would declare and any documented leak column removed, at
  least half of them chosen for a strong legitimate predictor.
- Each case is run as `benchmarks/evidence/leakage.py` runs it:
  `Project(..., leakage_policy="block").plan()` up to the leakage gate.

## Split

Acceptance. Both corpora are split by source table, so a table and every
leak planted on it fall on the same side, into development, validation
and acceptance by a seeded hash. The acceptance part is sealed (hash
recorded) before stage B and opened once by the validator in stage D.
The validation part decides the row in stages B and C.

## Statistic

Recall: cases where the screen blocked the run, carrying its `leakage`
evidence, over leak cases. False blocks: clean cases that did not reach a
model plan (blocked, refused by another gate, or crashed) over clean cases.

## Decision rule

Both must pass. Recall: `Floor(leak_recall_min = 0.90)` lower bound at the
Holm-adjusted `alpha`. False blocks: `Ceiling(false_block_max = 0.05)`
upper bound at the Holm-adjusted `alpha`. Holm is over the four stage-D
Clopper-Pearson rows (C2 recall, C2 false blocks, C14 rate, C14
precision). Fewer cases than the rule's minimum cannot pass.

## Sample size

Assumed true recall 0.97 (a screen worth shipping); assumed true
false-block rate 0.01. Sized at the first Holm step for the acceptance
split and at `alpha` for the validation split:

| Variable | Rule | True rate | Alpha | Minimum | Planned |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `leak_recall_min` | floor | 0.97 | 0.0125 | 42 | 124 |
| `false_block_max` | ceiling | 0.01 | 0.0125 | 86 | 192 |
| `leak_recall_min` | floor | 0.97 | 0.05 | 29 | 76 |
| `false_block_max` | ceiling | 0.01 | 0.05 | 59 | 124 |

So the acceptance split holds at least 124 leak cases and 192 clean
tables, and the validation split at least 76 and 124. The plan's "about
60 real leaks" and "about 150 clean datasets" are below these and are
superseded by them. Cases are clustered by source table; the binomial
bound treats them as independent, so no table contributes more than one
case per leak kind and the per-table breakdown is reported beside the
verdict.

## What counts as a failure

A leak case is missed when the run reaches a plan, or is stopped by
anything other than the screen. A clean case is a false block when it does
not reach a plan for any reason. A case whose table cannot be loaded
counts against the row (a miss or a false block), never drops out.

## Grader

The leak corpus is labelled (which column leaks, and why) by people not
working on the screen, before it is scored. The script is the committed
module; the validator opens the acceptance split.

## Where the current module differs

- Leaks are 6 documented real leaks (Titanic `boat` and `body`, bank
  marketing `V12`, NYC green taxi `total_amount`, Tecator `moisture` and
  `protein`) and 64 planted on 16 real tables, 16 of each kind (target
  encoding, post-outcome field, duplicated rows, future timestamp): 70,
  below the 124 planned and above the 42 the first Holm step needs. The
  builder labelled them. Tecator (OpenML 505) entered with the rule below;
  its description says moisture and protein were "determined by analytic
  chemistry", as fat, the target, was, so they were found to be documented
  leaks after the rule was fixed. Each is a leak case with the other
  removed, as Titanic's two are.
- Clean cases are 167 tables: 78 chosen by hand and 89 added by a rule
  fixed before any of them was screened (every real table of the C14
  catalog not already here). Five have their documented leak columns
  removed (bank marketing `V12`, energy heating `y2`, Titanic `boat` and
  `body`, NYC green taxi `total_amount`, Tecator `moisture` and
  `protein`). That is above the 86 the first Holm step needs and below the
  192 planned.
- No entity-level leak is planted.
- A future timestamp is screened as its user would run it, with the
  prediction time declared (`Dataset.cutoff`, the snapshot the closing
  dates fall after); the plan does not mention that declaration, and
  without it a closing date cannot be told from a legitimate trend.
- A documented real leak is screened with its availability declared
  (`Dataset.known`, column to `prediction` or `outcome`) only when its
  source's documentation says when the column becomes known relative to
  the prediction, and never from knowing the answer: bank marketing `V12`
  (the UCI page: "the duration is not known before a call is performed.
  Also, after the end of the call y is obviously known") and NYC green
  taxi `total_amount` (the TLC data dictionary: "the total amount charged
  to passengers", which includes the tip being predicted) and Tecator
  `moisture` and `protein` (OpenML 505: "real_out=3", outputs measured
  "by analytic chemistry" with the fat; see the note at the end). Titanic's
  leak columns are not declared. A feature declared `outcome` blocks the
  run whatever its statistics; the plan does not mention the declaration.
- The screen does not test duplicated records on a table whose columns'
  value space holds no more records than it has rows (an enumerated
  design such as monks), screens a table below its row minimum with a
  low-power note instead of leaving it unchecked, and judges record purity
  against the target's base rate, so a rare-class record leak on an
  imbalanced table can be missed (it then warns only).
- No ceiling was tuned to a clean table: sylvine (OpenML 41146) `V7`
  still blocks; it looks like the covertype elevation it derives from,
  but nothing documents it as a leak.
- A clean table the split gate refuses (a class too rare for the test rows)
  counts as a false block, as the plan words it; the row's outcomes count
  those apart from screen blocks.
- There is no split: every case is development. The module's
  `NOT_MEASURED` says so ("a sealed corpus").
- Holm is applied with the two C14 rows entered as not rejected (they are
  decided in `benchmarks/evidence/refusal.py`), which can only lower the C2
  levels.
- Its last recorded result, recall 66/70 (lower bound 0.847 against 0.90)
  and false blocks 7/167 (2 blocked by the screen, 5 refused by the split
  gate; upper bound 0.088 against 0.05), fails both rows. It was scored on
  development cases after this registration and decides nothing; the store
  was measured on commit afb58bd0, with a corpus of 70 leak cases that
  counts Tecator as a documented leak.
- Tecator's declaration was added on 2026-11-19, after the last recorded
  result showed `protein` missed, so it is a correction made after seeing
  a result. It rests on the source's own description, which lists the
  spectrum and its 22 principal components as the 122 inputs
  ("real_in=122") and moisture, fat and protein as the 3 outputs
  ("real_out=3"), all three "determined by analytic chemistry", the task
  being to "predict the fat content of a meat sample on the basis of its
  near infrared absorbance spectrum". An output measured by the analysis
  that measures the target is known with the outcome, which is what the
  rule asks the documentation to say; it was overlooked when the earlier
  text called Tecator's documentation silent.
