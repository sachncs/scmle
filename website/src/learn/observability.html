What scmle.operations does once a model is serving: measure it, tell a fault from a drift from a worse model, tell people, keep evaluating, replace it only through the gate, recover from failures, and decide whether the production claim may be made. Everything here ran locally; nothing ran on AWS or GCP. Evidence: plan/evidence/phase-17/local/. Feature: F14 items 11 to 13.

Telemetry

ServiceMetrics counts requests by route and status and observes latency. Availability is the share of requests that were not 5xx or 429; a 4xx the caller caused is available. Latency quantiles are estimated from histogram buckets, as Prometheus does, so they are exact only at bucket edges. SignalMonitor looks at a sliding window and reports, per signal, ok, breached or insufficient_data. A window with too few samples is never reported healthy. Feature rows are sampled at sample_rate; requests and outcomes are never sampled. Drift is the noise-adjusted PSI of scmle.monitoring.reference, so the thresholds are the ones the batch monitor already uses (MonitoringThresholds).

Classifying a fault

VerdictWhen
infrastructureOnly server errors, availability or latency are breached.
data_qualityOnly rejected inputs, missing-rate increase, unseen categories or drift are breached.
model_performanceOnly the loss over arrived labels is above the reference plus tolerance.
mixedBreaches of more than one kind.
indeterminateNo signal had enough data.

mixed is deliberate. A data fault can cause a performance drop and an outage can bias the labels that arrive, so the classifier does not rank them; it lists every breach and starts the responder at infrastructure, then data. Caveats travel with the verdict: drift before any label arrived "is not harm", and a performance drop with the data signals unjudged "does not exclude a data cause". Critical severity pages and files a ticket; warning files a ticket.

Notifications

Each webhook body is signed sha256=HMAC(secret, timestamp + "." + body) in X-Scmle-Signature, with X-Scmle-Timestamp and an idempotency key that is the same on every retry. A receiver calls notify.verify_signature. 5xx, 429 and transport errors retry with exponential backoff; other 4xx are final. A failed page is returned and written to the audit row, never dropped.

Continuous evaluation

Delayed labels become 0/1 losses and business outcomes become min(outcome / cap, 1) losses (the cap is declared by the metric's owner); both go to Loop.mature, for the serving model and for each challenger on the same cases. Raw labels and outcomes are kept in retained_outcomes.jsonl.

The loop's own alarm still does not gate retraining. alarm_gates_retrain stays False because the loop's detection-delay gates (H2, H10) missed their floors; the alarm records a row and opens an l1_alarm_investigate escalation, and trains nothing. Retraining comes from RetrainPolicy:

  • recommend (the default) opens a retrain_recommendation escalation with no_retrain as the proposed default. A challenger is trained only after a person's signed, allowing reply (run_approved_retrain).
  • trigger also trains a challenger at once and registers it with the loop. A challenger serves nothing until it wins.

A primary reason is required (loss above reference plus tolerance with enough outcomes, a loop alarm, or age). Drift supports a reason and is never enough alone. A proposal is held back while an infrastructure or data-quality fault is open, and during a cooldown.

A challenger is judged by the loop's e-process and e-LOND (online false discovery control over challengers); a win is a promotion_proposal row. ContinuousEvaluation.replace then refuses unless the loop proposed the challenger, the promotion gate admits the build (signed bundle, independent approval, passed scan, approver other than the requester, a signed staging record for production), an offline comparison, if given, says promote, and the release registry confirms the runtime. Dataset versions (content digest, with a preserved copy), training runs, proposals, replacements and checks are in a hash-chained training_history.jsonl.

post_promotion_check compares chosen operational and business metrics before and after with bootstrap intervals, split over the metrics so they hold together. A metric is improved or worse only when its whole interval is beyond the declared margin. The answer is helped, harmed or inconclusive, and it always says that two periods cannot separate the model from seasonality or other changes. The committed example is inconclusive.

Recovery

Objectives are declared before a run in recovery.OBJECTIVES and are not tuned to a result:

ComponentRTORPO (acknowledged units lost)Scope
service30 s0 answers that differrestart to ready
ledger10 s0 rowsreload and verify after the writer is killed
registry_state10 s0 writesatomic state-file writes killed at random
object_store30 s0 objectsthe local emulator, access restored to a verified bundle serving
secrets60 snone (held outside)detection of a credential mismatch to ready
telemetry_spool30 s0 samplesdestination back to spool drained (duplicates possible)
job_state60 s0 completed stepskilled run continued, lease wait included

faults.run_all() runs eleven faults against the real local service (the generated serve.py as a subprocess), the local object-store and metrics emulators, a real ledger writer and a real workflow executor, and records for each component the measured RTO and RPO and how they were measured. In bad_release the revisions are local processes behind a control plane that implements the ECS and Cloud Run adapter interface, so the release registry's promote, confirm, restore and rollback logic runs for real against them.

Not run: the rollback to a real managed revision, a zone or region loss, a container orchestrator's health-driven replacement, IAM or bucket-policy failures, a managed-service outage, secret-manager rotation, and on any machine a crash, power loss, full disk or network partition. The ledger and state files are shown to survive a killed process; no fsync guarantee against power loss is claimed.

The production claim

RELEASING.md holds the fixed sentence and says it has not been made. scripts/check_production_claim.py refuses it unless both providers' signed staging records are complete and a reviewer other than the author has signed them.

Not claimed

Anything on a cloud. The local scenarios measure these processes, emulators and files under the stated methods; they are not availability, not a cloud's behaviour, and not a recovery objective for a provider. The exporters were checked against shapes written from the API references, not against a service. The controlled degradation is a simulated stream with labels that arrive at once.
