The data and the question

target

Meaning. The one column you want to predict.

Example. survived (1 lived, 0 died).

Why it matters. Everything else is built around it. A wrong target means a perfect answer to the wrong question. See Decisions.

feature

Meaning. A column the model may use to guess the target.

Example. sex, age, pclass, fare.

Why it matters. The model can only learn from what you give it. A feature you would not have at prediction time is a leak (see below).

row, column, table

Meaning. A table has rows (one per thing, here one per passenger) and columns (one fact about each thing).

Example. 1309 rows and 10 columns.

Why it matters. The unit of a row ("one passenger", "one customer-month") decides how you must split the data. See "grouping" in Decisions.

task

Meaning. The kind of answer: a category (classification), a number (regression), a time until something happens (survival), and so on.

Example. binary_classification for two possible answers.

Why it matters. It decides which scores and models make sense. SCMLE works it out from the target and tells you in the plan; check it.

classification, regression

Meaning. Classification predicts a category. Regression predicts a number.

Example. "Lived or died" is classification. "Fare paid" is regression.

Why it matters. Using the wrong one gives meaningless scores.

missing value

Meaning. A cell with no recorded value (shown as NaN or empty).

Example. age is missing for 263 passengers.

Why it matters. Missing is often not random. That boat is missing for the people who died is a clue to a leak, not noise.

duplicate

Meaning. A row that is an exact copy of another.

Example. 234 of the Titanic rows are copies.

Why it matters. If one copy lands in the practice part and another in the exam part, the model has seen the exam answer. Scores come out too high.

Scores

metric

Meaning. The number that says how good the predictions are.

Example. ROC AUC, accuracy, RMSE.

Why it matters. The search picks the model that scores best on your metric. A metric that does not match your real goal picks the wrong model. See Decisions.

ROC AUC

Meaning. For a yes/no prediction: the chance that the model gives a higher score to a random "yes" than to a random "no". 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect.

Example. 0.885 means a random survivor is ranked above a random non-survivor 88.5 percent of the time.

Why it matters. It is the default for two-answer problems. It ignores the threshold (below), so it says nothing about how many mistakes you make at the cut you will actually use. A score above about 0.98 on real, messy data deserves suspicion.

accuracy

Meaning. The share of predictions that are right.

Example. 0.824: 82 of every 100 passengers guessed right.

Why it matters. It misleads when one answer is rare. If 1 in 100 cases is fraud, a model that says "never fraud" is 99 percent accurate and useless.

precision and recall

Meaning. Precision: of the ones you flagged, how many were truly "yes". Recall: of all the true "yes", how many you flagged.

Example. At a cut of 0.5, precision 0.78 and recall 0.76 for survivors.

Why it matters. They trade off against each other. Which matters more depends on the cost of each kind of mistake.

confusion matrix

Meaning. A 2 by 2 table of right and wrong guesses. Rows are the truth, columns are the guess.

Example. [[113, 18], [20, 65]]: 113 deaths right, 18 deaths guessed as lived, 20 survivors guessed as died, 65 survivors right.

Why it matters. It shows which kind of mistake you make, which a single number hides.

log loss, Brier score

Meaning. Scores that punish confident wrong probabilities. Lower is better.

Example. Log loss 0.408, Brier 0.127 in our run.

Why it matters. Use them when you will act on the probability itself, not only on the yes/no.

baseline, dummy model

Meaning. A trivial model to beat: one that ignores the features.

Example. The dummy model scores ROC AUC 0.5.

Why it matters. A model that does not clearly beat the baseline has learned nothing. Also compare against what your team does today (a "current process").

confidence interval (band)

Meaning. A range that the true value probably lies in, given that you only tested on a sample.

Example. Exam score 0.885, 95 percent band 0.841 to 0.927.

Why it matters. A small test set makes a wide band. Two models whose bands overlap are not clearly different.

calibration

Meaning. Whether stated probabilities match reality. Of all cases given 0.8, about 80 percent should happen.

Example. In our run the model said 0.14 for a group where 0.10 lived: close.

Why it matters. If you act on the number ("call everyone above 70 percent") it must mean what it says. Check diagnostics/calibration.json.

threshold (cut-off)

Meaning. The probability above which you say "yes".

Example. 0.5 by default. At 0.8, precision is 0.94 and recall 0.55.

Why it matters. It sets how many true cases you catch against how many false alarms you raise. Choose it from the cost of each mistake, not from habit.

PR AUC

Meaning. PR AUC is a similar score that focuses on the rare answer.

Example. 0.854 in our run.

Why it matters. Prefer it when "yes" is rare.

Honest testing

training data, practice rows

Meaning. The rows the model learns from.

Example. 1093 of our 1309 rows.

Why it matters. The model will always look good on rows it has already seen. Those scores say nothing about the future.

held-out data (exam rows, test set)

Meaning. Rows kept hidden until the end and used once to score the winner.

Example. 216 rows were hidden. SCMLE logs test.opened when it uses them.

Why it matters. It is the closest thing to an honest preview of real use. If you peek at it and then change the model, it stops being honest.

cross-validation (CV)

Meaning. Practice rounds. Split the practice rows into several parts, train on some, score on the rest, rotate, and average.

Example. 5 folds; the stacked model averaged 0.848 with a spread of 0.023.

Why it matters. It lets the search compare many models without touching the exam rows. The "cv" number is the practice score.

overfitting

Meaning. The model memorised the practice rows, quirks included, and does worse on new ones.

Example. A gap such as practice 0.99 and exam 0.70. SCMLE's fit_health says overfit for such trials; one of our trials was marked that way.

Why it matters. It is the most common reason a model that looked great in the lab fails in use.

underfitting

Meaning. The model is too simple to catch the pattern.

Example. Both practice and exam scores are poor.

Why it matters. Rarely the problem with this tool, but it looks like "the data has no signal".

leakage (leak)

Meaning. Information in a feature that you would not have when you really use the model, usually because it was recorded after the outcome.

Example. boat (which lifeboat) and body (body recovered) are only known after the sinking. With them the exam score was 0.988. Without them, 0.885.

Why it matters. It makes a model look brilliant and then fail. It is the single biggest cause of too-good results. See Reading results.

leakage suspect

Meaning. A feature SCMLE's statistical screen flags as probably a leak.

Example. boat and body.

Why it matters. It is a suspicion, not proof. A genuinely strong feature looks the same. A person must decide. See "unverified" and "attestation".

split

Meaning. How rows are divided into practice and exam parts.

Example. stratified: each part keeps the same share of survivors.

Why it matters. A wrong split lets exam information leak into practice. Related rows must stay on the same side (see "group" and "time order").

group, grouping

Meaning. Rows that belong together, such as all rows of one customer.

Example. --group-column customer_id.

Why it matters. If one customer is in both practice and exam, the model has effectively seen the exam. Scores come out too high.

time order, temporal split

Meaning. Training on the past and testing on the future.

Example. --time-column event_time.

Why it matters. Shuffled rows let the model learn from the future. A model for the future must be tested on the future.

dataset fingerprint

Meaning. A short code that identifies exactly these rows.

Example. split_fingerprint: 4bb647ae69c0aca6.

Why it matters. It proves that two runs used the same data and the same split.

seed, reproducible

Meaning. A seed is a starting number for randomness. Same seed, data and settings should give the same result.

Example. The command-line run and the Python run in Start here gave identical scores.

Why it matters. You can check someone else's result. Note that a time budget makes the search depend on computer speed.

Charter, custody and claims

These words belong to the careful, "pre-registered" way of working. You can build your first model without them. You need them when someone must be able to trust a finding, not just a model.

charter

Meaning. A written, signed plan made before looking at the data: the question, who the model is for, when it will be used, how many rows are held back and how much risk of a false finding you accept.

Example. "Can we predict from the ticket who survived? Population: passengers. Prediction moment: at boarding. Measure: ROC AUC."

Why it matters. It stops you changing the question after seeing the answer. A change to even one byte breaks its signature. Create one with scmle charter init.

estimand

Meaning. The exact thing you are estimating: who, what, when and how it is summarised.

Example. Population "passengers", target "survived", moment "at boarding".

Why it matters. Vague questions give answers that cannot be checked.

custody (X, C and F)

Meaning. Custody means the data is divided into locked sets with rules for who may look. X is the exploration set: you may look at it as much as you like. C is the confirmation set: sealed, opened exactly once, after you have decided what to test. F is the forward-in-time set, for data with dates: the newest rows, kept for a final check.

Example. In the Titanic charter run, 819 rows were X and 273 were C.

Why it matters. Anything you find by looking is at risk of being luck. Testing it once on rows you never looked at is the only way to know. The seal is held in a separate helper program so ordinary code cannot read C.

final test set

Meaning. The exam rows used to score the finished model (F for data with dates).

Example. 216 rows.

Why it matters. It is separate from C, so a confirmed finding never shapes the model that is scored.

hypothesis ledger

Meaning. A log of every idea tested on X, whether it passed, and how much "error allowance" it used.

Example. hypothesis_ledger.jsonl in a charter run.

Why it matters. If you try 100 ideas, a few will look good by luck. The ledger counts the tries so luck is not mistaken for discovery.

error budget, alpha

Meaning. How much risk of a false discovery you accept, stated up front.

Example. alpha_confirm of 1/20: about one in twenty confirmed findings may be false.

Why it matters. It is a promise about the confirmed list as a whole.

FDR (false discovery rate)

Meaning. The expected share of your "discoveries" that are false.

Example. With 3 confirmed claims at 5 percent FDR, you expect at most about 0.15 of one false claim.

Why it matters. It is the right yardstick when you test many ideas.

p-value

Meaning. How surprising the data would be if there were no real effect. Small means surprising. It is not the chance that you are right.

Example. 5e-09 for "fare is associated with survival".

Why it matters. Many tests make small p-values appear by chance, which is why the ledger and the error budget exist.

e-value

Meaning. A different evidence score: a number you can keep multiplying as data arrives, and which stays honest even if you look again and again.

Example. Used by the closed loop to decide whether a new model has beaten the old one, in a way that stays valid however often you check.

Why it matters. Ordinary tests break if you peek repeatedly. E-values do not, so they fit monitoring over time.

claim

Meaning. A finding that survived exploration on X and was then tested once on C.

Example. "fare is associated with survival", effect 0.344, confirmed.

Why it matters. Only claims, not hunches, carry the error guarantee. List them with scmle claims list.

tier (confirmed, attested, unverified)

Meaning. A label on every claim saying how much evidence stands behind it. confirmed: it passed the one-time test on fresh rows. attested: a named person vouched for a fact the data cannot show (for example, "this column is known before the outcome"). unverified: SCMLE used a default and nobody has checked it.

Example. In the Titanic charter run, the three claims are confirmed; the feature entries in admissibility_table.json are unverified ("availability at serving is unknown").

Why it matters. It tells you how far to trust a statement. unverified is not an error. It is an honest "nobody has checked this yet". See Reading results and When things go wrong.

attestation (attestor)

Meaning. A signed statement by a named person about something data cannot prove.

Example. "I confirm that column fare is known at boarding."

Why it matters. It moves a claim from unverified to attested, and makes a person responsible. SCMLE refuses an attestation signed with a key the system itself holds.

admissibility

Meaning. The check, per feature, of whether the model may use it: admit, admit_flagged (allowed with a warning) or quarantine (kept out).

Example. boat was quarantined; sex was admit_flagged.

Why it matters. It is the formal record of which columns you trusted.

causal claim

Meaning. A claim that changing X changes Y, not just that they move together.

Example. "Giving the discount raised sales" versus "customers who got the discount bought more".

Why it matters. Prediction does not tell you what would happen if you acted. A causal claim needs a design (an experiment or assumptions that someone names). SCMLE gives it a tier. Without a named person for each assumption, it stays unverified. A held-out set alone can never confirm a causal effect.

negative control

Meaning. A test where the answer is known to be "no effect", to see if the method wrongly finds one. SCMLE shuffles the target and re-runs the search.

Example. 20 shuffled re-runs found 0 discoveries. The real run found 3.

Why it matters. If shuffled data also gives discoveries, the method is fooling you.

certificate

Meaning. A measured record of how often a check is wrong, tested on data with planted problems.

Example. The leakage screen used a certified detector.

Why it matters. Only a check with a passing certificate may block a run. Any other check is shown but never stops anything ("descriptive").

gate

Meaning. A check that can stop a run or a release.

Example. The leakage block.

Why it matters. A gate is a decision, so it is only allowed to use checks whose error rates are known.

descriptive (versus certified)

Meaning. Output that informs but does not decide anything.

Example. A shift report that says "unverifiable".

Why it matters. Do not treat descriptive output as a guarantee.

conformal (prediction sets and intervals)

Meaning. A method that wraps any model's predictions in an interval or a set that is right at a promised rate, such as 90 percent.

Example. bundle.predict_interval(rows, coverage=0.9) for a number; bundle.predict_set(rows, coverage=0.9) for a category.

Why it matters. It tells you how unsure each single answer is. The promise holds when new data looks like the data used for calibration.

shift, drift

Meaning. Shift: the new data differs from the training data. Drift: the same thing happening over time in production.

Example. All fares multiplied by eight.

Why it matters. A model is only good for the world it learned.

PSI (population stability index)

Meaning. A number for how much one column's spread has moved. About 0.1 or less is calm, 0.25 or more is a strong shift.

Example. fare PSI 6.32 in the changed batch.

Why it matters. It is the main drift signal in the monitor.

Models and decisions

model

Meaning. A recipe that turns features into a prediction, learned from data.

Example. logistic_regression, random_forest, hist_gradient_boosting.

Why it matters. SCMLE tries many and keeps the best by cross-validation.

stacking, blending

Meaning. Combining several models into one. SCMLE uses the combination only if it beats its best single member.

Example. The Titanic winner was stacking.

Why it matters. It often adds a little accuracy at the cost of simplicity.

hyperparameter, tuning

Meaning. A model's setting chosen by you, not learned (such as tree depth). Tuning searches for good settings.

Example. n_estimators, max_samples.

Why it matters. It is why a time budget matters: more search can find better settings, at the cost of time.

feature importance (permutation importance)

Meaning. How much the score drops when one feature is shuffled.

Example. Shuffling sex cost 0.242 of AUC.

Why it matters. It shows what the model relies on. A surprising top feature is the first sign of a leak or a bug.

decision, cost of errors

Meaning. The action you take from a prediction and what each kind of wrong action costs.

Example. Missing a fraud costs 8; a false alarm costs 1.

Why it matters. It, not the metric, decides the right threshold. See Decisions.

net benefit

Meaning. A way to count the value of a decision rule that weighs true hits against false alarms at your costs.

Example. Compared against "treat everyone" and "treat no one".

Why it matters. A model that beats both across your plausible costs is useful; one that does not is not.

sensitive column

Meaning. A column such as sex or age group whose groups you want to compare for fairness.

Example. --sensitive-columns sex.

Why it matters. A good overall score can hide a poor one for a group. SCMLE reports the gap (see fairness.json and slice_performance.json).

Watching a model in use

monitoring

Meaning. Comparing production data and outcomes with what the model saw in training.

Example. ModelMonitor.assess.

Why it matters. Models fail quietly. Monitoring is how you hear about it.

delayed labels

Meaning. The true outcome arrives later than the prediction.

Example. You learn if a loan defaulted a year after approving it.

Why it matters. Performance can only be judged on rows whose outcome has arrived. The report shows the labeled fraction.

champion and challenger

Meaning. Champion: the model in use. Challenger: a candidate to replace it.

Example. v1 champion, v2 challenger.

Why it matters. The challenger must beat the champion on the same fresh rows before it takes over.

closed loop

Meaning. The cycle: watch, raise an alarm, retrain, test the challenger, promote only if evidence allows, and ask a person when unsure.

Example. scmle loop observe, loop step.

Why it matters. It automates the routine and keeps people for the hard calls. It is certified only in part (see What SCMLE cannot do).

escalation

Meaning. The system stops and asks a named person to decide.

Example. scmle loop pending lists open questions; scmle loop reply records the answer.

Why it matters. Some moves (for example moving the champion) must not be automatic. The answer is stored as an attested record.

halt, incident

Meaning. A halt stops the loop. An incident is the record of why.

Example. The loop starts halted if its state file does not match its ledger.

Why it matters. It is a safe stop that needs a person.

rollback

Meaning. Going back to the previous model.

Example. previous alias becomes champion again.

Why it matters. It is your undo. Test it before you need it.

Shipping and trust

bundle

Meaning. The saved model as a folder: the fitted model, its input schema, a reference profile for monitoring, and a manifest of file checksums.

Example. artifacts/titanic.

Why it matters. It is the unit you sign, register, serve and roll back.

schema

Meaning. The list of columns and types the model expects.

Example. pclass, sex, age, sibsp, parch, fare, embarked.

Why it matters. Wrong columns are refused, not guessed.

checksum (digest, hash)

Meaning. A short fingerprint of a file's exact contents. Change one byte and it changes.

Example. artifact_digest: sha256:17d1....

Why it matters. It proves two copies are identical.

signature (HMAC)

Meaning. A stamp made with a secret key. Anyone with the key can check that the content is unchanged and was stamped by a key holder.

Example. signature.json in the bundle.

Why it matters. Bundles can run code when opened, so SCMLE refuses an unsigned one. A signature proves the key, not the person; it is not a public certificate.

pickle, trusted

Meaning. Pickle is the file format of the model. Opening one can run code. --trusted tells SCMLE "I made this myself; open it anyway".

Example. scmle predict ... --trusted.

Why it matters. Never use --trusted on a model from someone else. It is refused outright when SCMLE_ENV=production.

SBOM (software bill of materials)

Meaning. A list of every library and version inside a package.

Example. sbom.cdx.json.

Why it matters. When a security flaw is announced, you can see in seconds whether you are exposed.

provenance

Meaning. A signed record of where something came from and what it was made of.

Example. provenance.json lists the bundle digest and build files.

Why it matters. The service checks it at start-up. A package without a valid one never becomes ready.

registry

Meaning. A shelf of numbered model versions with labels (champion, previous, challenger).

Example. registry/ with versions 1 and 2.

Why it matters. It makes "which model is live" a recorded fact, and moving the champion label needs a signed approval.

promotion, approval

Meaning. Promotion makes a version the champion. An approval is a signed note by an independent reviewer bound to one exact model.

Example. PromotionApproval(...).sign(key).

Why it matters. It stops one person pushing an unreviewed model into use.

model card

Meaning. A short description of a model's intended use, scores and limits.

Example. model_card.json in every run.

Why it matters. The next person should not have to guess what the model is for.

Cloud and operations

service, endpoint, liveness, readiness

Meaning. A service is a running program that answers requests. An endpoint is one address on it. Liveness (/livez) says it runs. Readiness (/readyz) says it is verified, loaded and can answer.

Example. /predict returns predictions.

Why it matters. A platform restarts a service that is not live and sends traffic only to one that is ready.

SLO (service level objective)

Meaning. A target for how the service should behave, set before measuring.

Example. 95th-percentile latency under 100 ms for one record.

Why it matters. It turns "fast enough" into a number you can check. See SERVICE_SLO.md.

latency, p95

Meaning. The time one request takes. p95 means 95 of every 100 requests are faster than this.

Example. p95_latency_ms: 29.7 for our model.

Why it matters. Averages hide slow outliers that users feel.

container, image

Meaning. An image is a frozen copy of a program and everything it needs. A container is a running image.

Example. Built from service/Dockerfile.

Why it matters. It runs the same way on every computer.

canary (staged traffic)

Meaning. Sending a small share of traffic to a new version first, watching, then sending more.

Example. 10 percent, then 50, then 100.

Why it matters. A bad release hurts few people and can be reversed. The staged-traffic code exists for Cloud Run; it is not yet wired into the production workflow (see CLOUD_RELEASE.md).

release gate

Meaning. The check that allows or refuses a release from its evidence.

Example. scmle deploy gate admit manifest.json --key-file key.

Why it matters. It refuses unsigned or unapproved models and lists every reason, and exits with code 2.

deployment record

Meaning. A signed record of what was deployed where, when, and who approved it.

Example. scmle deploy verify-record record.json.

Why it matters. It is your audit trail.

reconcile

Meaning. Comparing what the registry and ledger say with what the cloud really runs.

Example. scmle deploy reconcile.

Why it matters. It catches "the label says v1 but v2 is running".

RTO and RPO

Meaning. Recovery time objective: how long you may be down. Recovery point objective: how much recent data you may lose.

Example. RTO 30 minutes, RPO 5 minutes.

Why it matters. You prove you can recover within them before you claim production readiness.

SCMLE_SIGNING_KEY

Meaning. The environment variable holding your secret signing key.

Example. export SCMLE_SIGNING_KEY=...

Why it matters. Without it signed bundles cannot be opened. Keep it out of files and chat. Keys are read from files, not typed on a command line, in the deploy commands.
