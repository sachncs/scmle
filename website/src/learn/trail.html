A trail follows one deployed model through the records that already exist: the charter, the framing and decision-cost model, the claim ledger, the registry and release state, and the deployment ledger of the closed loop. It joins them into one folder that a reviewer who did not build the model can check with nothing else.

scmle trail build copies those records into a folder and seals it. scmle trail verify checks the folder against itself. scmle trail show prints it. scripts/trace_model.py is the same walk written for a reviewer: it imports nothing from SCMLE and reads nothing outside the folder.

What is in a trail

trail/
  trail.json                     ten steps, hash-chained, signed (analyst key)
  inputs.json                    the stakeholder's and the reviewer's statements
  reports/
    exploratory_report.json      what the exploration rows showed
    experiment_comparison.json   every candidate, ranked, with its spread
    outcome_tracking.json        observed cost per case against the expectation
  retained/
    run/         charter, claim ledger, custody log, evaluation, trials, bundle manifest
    framing/     framing draft, questions, decision-cost model
    registry/    registry.json, audit.jsonl, release_state.json
    deployment/  deployment_ledger.jsonl, loop_state.json, observed_outcomes.json

The ten steps, in order: the business question (the signed charter), framing and the cost model, KPIs with acceptance criteria and the approved expectation, the exploratory report, the experiment comparison, domain quality thresholds with the reviewer's evidence, model selection with the claim ledger and the bundle identity, deployment approval and release state, the production decisions in the deployment ledger, and the observed outcomes against the approved expectation.

Who says what

inputs.json has two sections, each signed with its author's own HMAC key (scmle.contracts.signing, the signing every other record uses):

  • the stakeholder maps each business KPI to a metric, writes the acceptance criteria (path, op, value, reason) and approves an expectation: a range for the observed cost per case, with its basis;
  • the reviewer states the domain quality thresholds and signs the evidence of the review, which names each record read and its digest. A record that changes afterwards is reported.

The charter is signed by the analyst, and the promotion is a PromotionApproval signed with the reviewer's key. The trail is signed by the analyst over its head and the digest of every retained record. A signature proves possession of a key, not identity.

What verify detects

Each finding has a kind. Every one has a seeded test in tests/unit/test_trail.py.

KindMeaning
missing_linkA record, a step or an input the trail names is absent, or no outcome can be compared.
broken_chainA step does not continue the hash chain, or the deployment or claim ledger does not chain.
unsigned_stepThe charter, the stakeholder or reviewer section, the approval or the trail carries no signature that verifies.
unapproved_stepNo signed approval promoted this version, it was for another bundle, rejected, not independent, or made by the builder.
outcome_outside_expectationThe 95% interval of the observed cost per case does not overlap the approved range.
record_tamperedA retained file differs from its sealed digest, or changed after the reviewer signed.
fact_mismatchA stored fact or derived report is not what the retained records give now.
identity_mismatchTwo records name different charters, bundles or runs.
criterion_not_metAn acceptance criterion or quality threshold fails on the retained numbers.

verify re-derives every fact from the retained records; it does not trust the numbers in trail.json. The exploratory report and the experiment comparison use no random draw and no clock, so recomputing them gives the same bytes and the same sha256.

Observed outcomes and what they do not show

The cost of a case is the cell of the chartered cost model for the action the cut takes and the outcome that arrived. The report gives the mean cost per case with a normal-approximation 95% interval and compares the interval, not the mean, with the approved range: within, above, below, insufficient (fewer than 100 matured cases) or absent. It says in words that the interval is not a forecast and that a cost inside the range does not show the model caused the outcome, because no counterfactual was observed. The cost is that of the rule on every case; cases the loop escalated to a person are counted by the deployment ledger and still costed here.

The local evidence and its limits

plan/evidence/phase-18/local/ holds a trail for one model, produced by scripts/make_phase18_evidence.py with the real commands: scmle frame, charter sign, run, package, the local registry, scmle loop, then trail build. Read these limits before relying on it.

  • The analyst, stakeholder and reviewer were all the author, with three different keys. The reviewer-independence check compares names, and the names are role labels. No independent person has traced or signed this trail. The gate "a reviewer who did not build the model traces one deployed model" is open.
  • The Titanic extract has no key and no timestamp. The framing step used a row number as the unit and a constant boarding date as the prediction moment, both assumptions. Because the extract is ordered, the row number correlates with the target, and the claim ledger tested it.
  • The observed outcomes replay the held-out test rows through the loop. They agree with the expectation by construction, so they show the mechanism and not the model's cost in use. The cost ceiling was set after the held-out evaluation was seen.
  • The confirmation set was smaller than the chartered power needs, so the run's scope is exploratory_unconfirmed; the trail carries that fact.
  • The release state is approved, not production: no runtime confirmed a deployment. Nothing ran on AWS or GCP.

Reviewing a trail

python scripts/trace_model.py trail --keys keys.json
scmle trail verify trail --keys keys.json

keys.json maps analyst, stakeholder and reviewer to the keys that verify them. The keys in the committed evidence are published demonstration keys: they show tamper evidence, not a statement by their holder. Without keys the script exits 2 and says which signatures it did not check.
